# The Golden Gate Bridge: history and engineering

This summary was written for this exercise from widely published public facts. Every figure below is the one to use. Dimensions are given in metres, with the common imperial figure in brackets.

## Overview

The Golden Gate Bridge is a suspension bridge that crosses the Golden Gate, the narrow strait where San Francisco Bay meets the Pacific Ocean. It links the city of San Francisco, on the tip of its peninsula, with Marin County to the north. It carries a six-lane road, a footpath and a cycle path.

From the day it opened in 1937 until 1964 it had the longest main span of any suspension bridge in the world. It is painted a colour called International Orange, which was chosen so that the structure stands out against the sea and the fog and is easy for ships to see.

## Timeline

- 1933: construction begins on 5 January.
- 1935: the two main cables begin to be spun between the towers.
- 1937: the bridge opens to pedestrians on 27 May and to vehicles the next day.
- 1964: the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge in New York opens with a longer main span, ending the Golden Gate Bridge's record.

## Engineering

The bridge hangs its deck from two main cables that pass over two steel towers. The towers rise 227 m (746 ft) above the water. The main span, the distance between the towers, is 1,280 m (4,200 ft), and each of the two side spans is 343 m (1,125 ft). The total length of the bridge, including its approaches, is 2,737 m (8,981 ft). The deck is 27 m (90 ft) wide and is 67 m (220 ft) above the water at the middle of the span.

Each main cable is 92.4 cm (36.4 in) thick and is made of 27,572 steel wires that were spun in place and then bound together. The deep currents and strong winds of the strait shaped the design, so the deck is built to flex and to carry wind loads into the towers and cables.

## Construction and safety

The towers stand on foundations built in deep, fast moving water, which was among the hardest parts of the job. A safety net hung under the deck while the roadway was built. The net saved the lives of 19 workers. Eleven workers died during construction, most of them in a single accident in 1937 when a work platform fell through the net.

## Cost

The bridge cost about 35 million dollars to build. It was paid for by bonds, which were repaid from the tolls collected on the bridge.
